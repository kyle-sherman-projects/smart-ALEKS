Rapid Cycle Evaluation Overview

LearnPlatform's Research team prepared and ran rapid cycle evaluations on i-Ready using the following research designs:

  1. Association of time on task with i-Ready's internal diagnostic scores, Kindergarten through 12th grade, for the 2025–2026 academic year
  2. Association of time on task with SmarterBalanced state summative scores, run separately for each tested grade (3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, and 10)
Summary Results
+

Students in grades Kindergarten through 8th who spent more time on task in i-Ready Math had higher diagnostic scores. This same relationship held up on the state summative test in grades 3, 4, 7, and 8 — two independent measures agreeing is a stronger signal than either alone.

+

The relationship was strongest in grades 3 and 4, where both the internal diagnostic and the state test agreed, in both Math and ELA. It faded through the middle grades and was not significant by high school.

+

Across all grades, 14,081 students were included in the district-wide diagnostic analysis. No usage/dosage goal was configured for this trial, so this brief reports average time on task rather than a compliance rate.

Rapid Cycle Evaluation Overview

LearnPlatform by Instructure (LearnPlatform), in collaboration with Sample School District, conducted a total of 16 Rapid Cycle Evaluations (RCEs) to examine the relationship between i-Ready usage and both its internal diagnostic and SmarterBalanced state summative scores.1 The diagnostic-based RCEs were designed to satisfy Level III requirements (Promising Evidence) according to the Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA) (see Appendix A for more information on ESSA), and results from both outcome measures were compared for agreement wherever both were available at the same grade.

1 LearnPlatform by Instructure's RCE reports are generated using IMPACT 2.0. This software supports a range of research designs and methods that enable systematic examinations of product usage and outcomes within diverse educational contexts.

Summary of Impacts

The RCEs used descriptive statistics to support usage analyses and partial correlations2 to examine how time on task relates to outcomes. Findings indicate that students in grades Kindergarten through 8th who spent more time on task in i-Ready Math had higher diagnostic scores. The same relationship faded by grade 9 and was not statistically significant in grades 9 or 10.

Next Steps

Appendix A

The Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA) provides schools and districts with a framework for determining which products are evidence-based and have been shown to improve student or other relevant outcomes. LearnPlatform classifies the research of interventions into one of four ESSA evidence tiers.

IV
ESSA LEVEL IV
Demonstrates Rationale
  • Research-based logic model for why this product should work
  • Blueprint for implementation with fidelity
  • Not empirical research — a rationale, not a finding
III
ESSA LEVEL III
Promising Evidence
  • Correlational study showing a positive relationship between usage and outcomes
  • No comparison group, random assignment, or baseline equivalence
  • Most meaningful for districts with similar context
II
ESSA LEVEL II
Moderate Evidence
  • Quasi-experimental study showing users outperformed non-users
  • Demographically similar comparison group, not randomly assigned
  • District context should be strongly considered
I
ESSA LEVEL I
Strong Evidence
  • Experimental study proving users outperformed non-users
  • Randomized comparison group for highly generalizable evidence
  • Establishes eligibility for all types of federal funding

Appendix C
  1. What could be the reason certain grades or school sites have higher usage or more positive effectiveness?
    1. Are there best practices in the strongest-showing schools or grades (3rd and 4th) that could be replicated elsewhere?
    2. Are there barriers to implementation that could explain lower usage in grades 9–10?
    3. Did some sites receive different professional learning opportunities?
  2. What does success look like for this product's implementation?
    1. Right now, is usage the most important part of implementation, or should the focus shift to effectiveness once a dosage goal is set?
  3. How can we use these results within our district?
    1. Should a usage/dosage goal be defined and communicated to teachers, since none is currently configured?
    2. Can implementation leaders from the strongest-showing grades support other grades?
  4. Can we work with LearnPlatform to iterate on these results — for example, closing the SmarterBalanced grade 10 data-matching gap, or running more specific reports by school or classroom?
