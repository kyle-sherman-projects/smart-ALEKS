RCE overview

This brief evaluates i-Ready, a K–12 personalized reading and math platform, across Bellevue School District's full student population during the 2025–26 school year. Two outcome measures were assessed independently: i-Ready's own internal diagnostic, scored district-wide in one analysis per subject (ELA, Math), and Washington State's SmarterBalanced summative test, administered only in grades 3 through 8 and grade 10, with a separate analysis run for each tested grade. Usage is measured as time on task, tracked separately for ELA and Math. This is the first Research Brief for this engagement in this repository, so it serves as the baseline for future comparison.

Flagged patterns & anomalies
These patterns come from scanning across grades, subjects, and both outcome measures at once. Each states what the data shows, which assessment(s) it comes from, why it stands out, and the question it raises — never a causal claim.
1
The usage–outcome relationship is strongest in the earliest grades, and two independent assessments agree on it

In Math, both the iReady diagnostic and SmarterBalanced show significant positive effects in grades 3 and 4 — the only two grades where both assessments were run and both came back significant. In ELA, only 3rd grade earns that same double confirmation.

Math, grade 3: both significant Math, grade 4: both significant ELA, grade 3: both significant

Two independently run outcome measures, one internal to the product, one a state summative test, agreeing on the same grades and direction is a stronger signal than either measure alone. This isn't true anywhere else in the data.

Is there something about how 3rd- and 4th-grade classrooms use i-Ready, more consistent scheduling, closer teacher involvement, better content fit, that could be extended to other grades?

2
Math shows a broader, more durable effect than ELA on the same platform

On the iReady diagnostic, Math is significant in every grade from Kindergarten through 8th. ELA is only significant Kindergarten through 3rd grade, and turns not significant from 4th grade on. The same pattern shows up in SmarterBalanced: Math is significant in four of seven tested grades (3, 4, 7, 8); ELA in only two (3, 4).

Math (iReady): significant K–8th ELA (iReady): significant only K–3rd

Same platform, same district, same time period — the subject-level gap isn't explained by anything external to how the product is used for each subject.

Is ELA content within i-Ready used or delivered differently than Math content in this district, and would a closer look at ELA-specific usage patterns (not just total time on task) explain why the effect fades after 3rd grade?

3
Grade 10's SmarterBalanced result is a data problem, not a finding

Grade 10's SmarterBalanced sample looks similar to every other grade at a glance (1,684 students in scope), but only 52 had a matched score to pair with usage data — versus roughly 1,300–1,900 matched students in every other tested grade.

Grade 10: 52 of 1,684 matched Every other tested grade: 1,300–1,900 matched

Reporting this alongside grades 5 and 6 (which have full-size samples and are genuinely not significant) would misrepresent it as the same kind of result. It isn't — it's inconclusive due to missing data, not evidence the relationship disappears at grade 10.

What's preventing more grade 10 students from having a matched SmarterBalanced score — a scheduling gap, a reporting delay, or something else — and can it be resolved before the next reporting period?

Data notes
Data notes & flags
  • An assessment run one grade at a time produces one row per grade in the source database — all of them belong in this brief. SmarterBalanced is only administered in grades 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, and 10 in Washington State (not 9, 11, or 12), and each grade is its own analysis rather than one combined model.
  • Two outcome measures, two different grains — always labeled by source, never merged into one verdict. The iReady diagnostic is one district-wide K–12 analysis; SmarterBalanced is seven separate single-grade analyses. The "Significant results by grade" table above states explicitly whether a significant finding came from iReady, SmarterBalanced, or both, rather than reporting a single combined significance call.
  • Grade 10 SmarterBalanced has a data-matching problem, not a null effect. Only 52 of 1,684 in-scope students had a matched score, versus full-size samples in every other tested grade.
  • No dosage goal configured. recommended_use / target_usage is 0 for this trial. Average usage is reported; a compliance percentage is not, since there's no real threshold to compare against.
  • An unusually large effect size on a small sample. Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islander students show r = +0.76 (95% CI: 0.47–0.90) on the iReady Math diagnostic, at n = 29 — right at the edge of the n < 30 stability threshold used elsewhere in this pipeline. Flagging as unstable rather than headlining it.
  • Some rows return no effect size despite adequate sample size. 11th and 12th grade (iReady Math) and two schools (Bellevue Digital Discovery Secondary, n=276; Bellevue Open Doors, n=91) all show a null effect size with no clear cause visible in the data. Flagging rather than guessing at a reason.
