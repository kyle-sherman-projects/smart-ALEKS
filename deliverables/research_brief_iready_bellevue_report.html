Bellevue School District evaluated i-Ready against two outcome measures covering the 2025–26 school year: its own internal diagnostic (scored district-wide, K–12) and Washington State's SmarterBalanced summative test (administered separately in grades 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, and 8, and 10). Both measures tell a consistent story: the relationship between time on task and outcomes is strongest and most reliable in the earliest grades, fades through upper elementary and middle school, and is inconclusive by grade 10 due to a data-matching problem specific to that grade, not because the effect disappears. No dosage/usage goal was configured for this trial, so this brief reports average usage without a compliance percentage.

Grade 10's SmarterBalanced result needs a different caveat than the rest
Grade 10's sample size column looks similar to every other grade (1,684 students in scope), but only 52 actually had a matched SmarterBalanced score to pair with usage data — versus roughly 1,300–1,900 matched students in every other tested grade. That's a data-availability problem, not evidence the relationship disappears at grade 10.

Data notes & flags
  • An assessment run one grade at a time produces one row per grade in the source database — all of them belong in this brief. SmarterBalanced is only administered in grades 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, and 10 in Washington State (not 9, 11, or 12), and each grade is its own analysis rather than one combined model.
  • Two outcome measures, two different grains. The iReady diagnostic is one district-wide K–12 analysis; SmarterBalanced is seven separate single-grade analyses. They are presented as separate result sets above, never merged into one table.
  • Grade 10 SmarterBalanced has a data-matching problem, not a null effect. Only 52 of 1,684 in-scope students had a matched score, versus full-size samples in every other tested grade.
  • No dosage goal configured. recommended_use / target_usage is 0 for this trial. Average usage is reported; a compliance percentage is not, since there's no real threshold to compare against.
  • An unusually large effect size on a small sample. Native Hawaiian or Other Pacific Islander students show r = +0.76 (95% CI: 0.47–0.90) on the iReady Math diagnostic, at n = 29 — right at the edge of the n < 30 stability threshold used elsewhere in this pipeline. Flagging as unstable rather than headlining it.
  • Some rows return no effect size despite adequate sample size. 11th and 12th grade (iReady Math) and two schools (Bellevue Digital Discovery Secondary, n=276; Bellevue Open Doors, n=91) all show a null effect size with no clear cause visible in the data. Flagging rather than guessing at a reason.
